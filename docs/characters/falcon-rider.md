# Falcon Rider (`falcon-rider`)

Air specialist. Spear. Ivory white with gold (alternate: charcoal with emerald). Motive: the storm took his falcon's nesting cliffs, and he wants them brought down to earth again. Rival: Sea Siren.

## 1. Name options

| # | English | Arabic | Meaning and fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nawaf** | نواف | "The lofty one, the one who stands high." A familiar Gulf name that says height without naming the bird. |
| 2 | Saqr | صقر | "Falcon." Strong and instantly understood, but it gives the rider the bird's name and blurs the two characters. |
| 3 | Rakan | راكان | "Steadfast, dependable." A good falconer's virtue, but nothing in it points to the sky. |

**Recommended: Nawaf (نواف).** It is a real, well-loved name across the Gulf, easy to say in English, and its meaning (high, overlooking) fits a cliff falconer who fights from above. It also leaves the bird with her own name, which matters to falconers: the falcon is a partner, not an accessory.

**Title:** Falconer of the Heights / صقار الأعالي

**Gender:** male. A young man in his early twenties. This also balances the roster at two men (Djinn Warrior, Falcon Rider), two women (Sea Siren, Dagger Dancer) and one creature (Dune Ghoul).

**The falcon:** **Muzna (مزنة)**, "rain cloud." A female saker falcon (in Gulf falconry the female saker is called الحر, and the female is the larger hunting bird). She carries the blood of the Roc (الرخ) from the Sindbad tales.

## 2. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Archetype | Air specialist |
| Weapon | Long spear (رمح) with a broad leaf blade, plus Muzna, his falcon |
| Difficulty | 3 of 3 |
| Playstyle | Nawaf fights the way a falcon hunts: climb high with three air jumps, wait, then stoop straight down onto the opponent with a spear spike or a wing spin. On the ground he is ordinary at best, so every good round is spent above the opponent's head. |

**Strengths**
1. Best air mobility in the roster: 3 air jumps (the only fighter allowed 3), the highest jump, the fastest air speed, plus the Stoop, which turns any spare jump into an instant dive.
2. Strongest aerials: a fast, wide Wing Wheel, a true spike in Falling Spearhead, and an up light that juggles.
3. Very hard to edgeguard: four air actions plus a recovery give him many ways home, and the Stoop changes his fall timing at will.

**Weaknesses**
1. The lightest fighter (weight 90): he flies farther and stays in hitstun longer, so he loses stocks early.
2. Weak ground game: no lunge on side light, low ground damage, and 22 frames of recovery on both heavies.
3. The Stoop is committal: 4 locked frames in a straight line, 8 frames of landing lag if he lands without attacking, and it spends the jumps he needs to recover. A careless Stoop off the stage is a self destruct.

## 3. Lore

**Bio**

Nawaf grew up on the high cliffs above the sands, where the falconers' families kept the oldest eyrie in the land. The cliff folk tell that the great Roc of Sindbad's tales once nested on those ledges, and that its line never truly left: every few generations a falcon hatches there with wings too wide for her body and gold at the tips of her flight feathers. Muzna is the latest. Nawaf found her as a fallen chick after a storm night, fed her from his own hand and trained her the old way: the hood to calm her, the glove to carry her, the lure to call her home, and patience above everything.

The cliff falconers keep a promise to their birds. When the hunting season ends, the falcons are set free to nest. Muzna always came back.

Then the oath beneath the sands cracked again, and the storm tore the high cliffs out of the earth and carried them into the Drift. The eyrie now drifts somewhere inside the storm, too high and too wild for any bird to raise young. Muzna has nowhere to nest, and the Roc's line will end with her unless the cliffs come down.

So on the Night of Suhail, Nawaf climbs to the calm ring with a spear in his hands and Muzna on his shoulder. He is quiet, polite and a little proud, and he talks to his falcon more than to people. He fights the way she hunts: rise high, wait, then stoop.

**His claim on the storm:** set the high cliffs back on the earth where they stood, so that Muzna, and every falcon after her, has a home to return to.

## 4. Visual design

### How the falcon appears (the size decision)

A rider mounted on a giant bird would be two to three times the size of the other fighters, would hide the hurtbox inside a bird shape, and could never stand on a platform with readable feet. So Nawaf is a normal fighter-sized man on foot, and **Muzna rides on him**:

- **Perched on his rear shoulder** (the shoulder farther from the opponent) on a padded leather shoulder guard. Folded, she is about a quarter of his height and sits just behind and slightly above his head line. This gives the silhouette its signature "two heads" top and keeps the front of the body, where the spear works, clean.
- **Her wings become his wings.** In the moves where the Roc blood shows (Wing Wheel, Skyward Thrust, Muzna's Lift, the taunt and the victory), she opens her wings from the shoulder. Spread, her wingspan is about equal to his height, far wider than any real falcon: that is how the Roc heritage reads at 64 px without making her huge all the time.
- **She leaves the shoulder only in the taunt and victory**, and only to step onto the glove on his rear forearm, staying within a body length. She never flies free in a gameplay sheet: AutoSprite keeps the character in place, and a detached bird would drift between frames, break art QA and blur where the hitbox is.
- **The hood (برقع) is gold and hangs from his belt.** A falcon is hooded to keep her calm and unhooded for the hunt, so she fights unhooded and alert. In the victory he slips the hood back on: the hunt is over. This is the falconer's ritual done right, not a costume detail.
- **She is never hurt.** Muzna is not part of the hurtbox, never takes knockback, is never thrown as a weapon and never strikes with her own body as the damaging part. In hurt frames she grips and flares her wings for balance. The damage always comes from the spear.

### Why the black silhouette is different from the other 4

Judged against the canon seeds: the Djinn Warrior reads as a tall turban with a flowing tail and a short curved blade; the Dune Ghoul as a hunched, wide, low mass with big claws; the Sea Siren as a round returning weapon and sea shapes; the Dagger Dancer as a slim figure with two small blades. Nawaf is the only shape with **a long straight line** (the spear crossing the body diagonally), **two bumps at the head line** (his low head wrap plus the falcon behind it), and **a saw-toothed cape edge** that opens into wing shapes in the air. In black he reads as narrow and vertical with a lance and a bird, which no other fighter has. In the air the shape changes most of all, since Muzna's spread wings double his width: the roster's air specialist is visibly winged.

Readability notes: ivory sits close to the noon sandstone of Hejaz Ruins, so the 3 px outline, the brown leather and falcon masses, and the gold trim must carry the value contrast there. Charcoal sits close to the night of Djinn Market, which is why the alternate keeps pale grey trousers and white highlights.

## 6. Hook: The Stoop (الانقضاض)

A falcon's stoop is a folded-wing dive from height, the fastest move in nature. Nawaf can spend any air jump to **stoop instead of rising**: every wingbeat can go up or down.

### Rules

**Input.** In the air, in the idle state (not attacking, not in hitstun, not dodging), with at least 1 air jump left: hold **Down** and press **Jump**. A buffered Jump (the 7-frame buffer) counts. On the ground, Down + Jump is a normal jump. Without Down held, Jump is a normal air jump.

**Start (stoop frame 0).**
- `jumps -= 1` (jumpCost 1, the Stoop is an air jump)
- `stp = 18` (stoopFrames)
- `vy = 2200` (stoopVy, downward)
- `vx = d * 600` if a side is held (stoopVx, diagonal dive), else `vx = trunc(vx / 2)`
- `ff = 1`, `cut = 1`, and face the held side if any

**Each frame while `stp > 0`.** Decrement `stp`, then let `k = 18 - stp` (frames since the start).
- `vy` is held at 2200: gravity is skipped and the fall cap is not applied while `stp > 0`.
- `vx` is locked: air drift input is ignored.
- `k` 1 to 4 (commitFrames 4): no actions. Presses stay in the 7-frame buffer, so an attack pressed during the commit comes out on frame 5.
- `k` 5 or more: any legal air attack (neutral air, side light, up light, down air, ground pound, recovery if unused) or air dodge. Starting one sets `stp = 0`. The attack keeps the dive speed, capped at fast fall 2040 because `ff = 1`.
- `k` 8 or more (jumpCancelFrame 8): an air jump, or a new Stoop, if jumps remain. Either sets or resets `stp` as usual.
- When `stp` reaches 0 with no action taken, he falls normally with fast fall still on.

**Landing.** If he lands while `stp > 0` (a pure Stoop, no attack), set `stp = 0` and `slag = 8` (landLag). While `slag > 0` he stays grounded and idle, decrements `slag` each frame, takes no actions, ignores run input and slides with ground friction. Buffered presses survive, so a press near the end comes out on the first free frame. Landing during an attack started from a Stoop follows the normal rule: aerials cancel on landing.

**Reset.** `stp = 0` and `slag = 0` when he is hit, when he dies and on respawn. Normal landing refills jumps as today.

**Distances.** The 4 locked frames cover 88 px. An uncancelled Stoop covers about 396 px in 18 frames, which is deep enough to lose a stock off the stage.

### Integer state fields

| Field | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `stp` | 0 to 18 | Stoop frames left. 0 means not stooping. |
| `slag` | 0 to 8 | Stoop landing lag frames left. |

Parameters (from `hook` in moves.draft.json): stoopFrames 18, commitFrames 4, jumpCancelFrame 8, stoopVy 2200, stoopVx 600, landLag 8, jumpCost 1. No cooldown timer is needed: the cost is the air jump itself, refilled only on landing (or the minimum 1 jump every fighter gets when hit).

### Counterplay

- The 4 committed frames are a straight line at a fixed speed: up-angled anti-airs (up lights, recoveries) placed under his path win cleanly.
- A Stoop that lands without attacking gives 8 frames of lag: any light with startup 5 or less punishes it on reaction to the landing dust.
- Every Stoop spends an air jump. Count his jumps (the HUD shows them as feather pips): a Nawaf with 0 jumps left can only fall or use his recovery.
- Off the stage, a deep Stoop has to be paid back with jumps. Chase him low after he stoops; he is light and dies early.
- He can feint (Stoop, then jump at frame 8), so do not throw out the anti-air too early: wait for the frame 5 attack or the landing.

### Why it is rollback safe

- Two new integer fields, `stp` and `slag`, live in the fighter object created by `u()`. The sim builds the clone and hash key list from `Object.keys(u(0, 1))`, so both fields are saved, restored and hashed on every rollback with no extra code.
- The rules read only that frame's input bits and the fighter's own state. No floats (`vx / 2` uses `Math.trunc`), no randomness, no clock, no new entity or projectile.
- All parameters come from the hashed moves.json, so both peers refuse to start if the data differs.
- The renderer's dive trail and landing dust are pure functions of `stp` and `slag` and never write to the sim.

## 8. Game plan

Combo math uses hitstun `6 + floor(l / 48)` with `l` after the victim's weight (victim weight 100 below; lighter victims take them earlier). A follow-up is true when his frames left after the hit, plus 1 frame to jump if needed, plus the next move's startup, is less than the hitstun. Hit pause freezes both fighters and does not change this.

### Bread-and-butter combos

1. **Stoop Drop (0% to about 110%).** Stoop over a grounded foe, Falling Spearhead, land, Skyward Thrust. The spike on a grounded foe becomes a small bounce. The down air cancels on landing, and he lands about 2 to 3 frames after the hit, so the up light hits around frame 7 against 13 frames of hitstun at 0% (19 at 100%). Above about 110% the bounce and the 30 sideways push carry them out of the up light box. 19% total.
2. **Wing Ladder (from about 30%, ender from about 70%).** Wing Wheel, air jump, Wing Wheel, air jump, Long Thrust. Jump into them so the Wing Wheel connects on its 4th active frame or later: he is free 10 frames after the hit, plus 1 to jump, plus 3 startup is 14, which needs `l` 432 (from 26%). The Long Thrust ender needs 17 frames of hitstun, `l` 528, so it links from about 74% on the second Wing Wheel. Spends 2 air jumps and carries the foe toward the side blast zone. 16% or 23%.
3. **Sky Ladder (from about 40%).** Skyward Thrust, jump, Skyward Thrust in the air. With a last-active-frame hit he is free after 10 frames, plus 1 plus 4 is 15, which needs 16 frames of hitstun, `l` 480, so from 40%. A normal mid-active hit links from about 88%. Repeat with the next air jump at higher damage. 16% per pair.

### Kill setups

1. **Ledge spike.** When the foe recovers low, step off the ledge, Stoop toward them and Falling Spearhead straight down. The spike angle (30, 95) sends them under the stage, and their only jump back is the 1 they get when hit. Expect kills from about 60% after the global retune, earlier against foes who used their jumps. Only go deep with 2 or more air jumps left.
2. **Stoop feint into Roc Lance, or Sky Ladder off the top.** Near the ledge, Stoop at a grounded foe and jump-cancel at frame 8, out of their anti-air. When the anti-air whiffs, fast fall and land with Roc Lance (K 1080) for a side kill from about 130% at the ledge. Against a foe who sits still instead, Stoop Drop into Sky Ladder takes them off the top from about 130%.

### How to play against the Falcon Rider

- Take the ground and stay under him. His ground moves are weak and his heavies recover in 22 frames: make him land.
- Watch the Stoop. The first 4 frames are a fixed straight line: anti-air into the path. If he lands without attacking, punish the 8 frames of landing lag with your fastest light.
- Count feathers. Each Stoop uses one of his 3 air jumps; with 0 left he is predictable.
- Hit him early and often. At weight 90 he flies 11% farther and sits in hitstun longer, so your combos run longer on him than on anyone else.
- Chase him low, not high. He comes home high with jumps to spare, but a deep Stoop off the stage leaves him short.

## 12. Sim changes required

1. **Per-fighter data.** Load `stats`, `hook` and `moves` from each fighter's hashed moves.json instead of the global `PHY` and `MOVES`. Each fighter object needs a reference to its own constants (by roster index, an integer, so it stays in the hashed state).
2. **Per-fighter physics.** Derive run, airMax, airAcc, jumpV, airJumpV, jumpCut, grav, maxFall and fastFall from the percentages once at load, with integer math (round half up) and an integer square root for jump height. Hitstun gravity and hitstun max fall stay global.
3. **3 air jumps.** `airJumps` per fighter, used on spawn, respawn and landing (today these read the global `l.airJumps`). The HUD shows remaining jumps as feather pips (renderer only).
4. **Weight.** On hit, `l = floor(l * 100 / victimWeight)` before hitstun, velocity and the hit effect size are computed.
5. **The Stoop.** New fighter fields `stp` and `slag` added to `u()` (so clone and hash pick them up). New rules: the Down + Jump air branch, the 4-frame action lock, attack and dodge cancels from frame 5, jump cancels from frame 8, held dive velocity with gravity and the fall cap skipped, the landing lag, and resets on hit, death and respawn. Today jumps and attacks are only read in the idle state, so the stoop windows need an explicit check there.
6. **Fall cap and `iy` (affects every fighter).** Today a move's downward `iy` is clipped by the fall cap on the same frame (ground pound `iy` 2100 becomes 1050, or 1700 when fast falling). Please exempt `endOnLand` moves from the cap while active, so Roc Stone's `iy` 2300 means what it says. Without that change it falls at his fast fall 2040 or max fall 1260.
7. **Input mapping (affects every fighter).** Today plain B in the air starts the side heavy and Down + B in the air starts the ground pound, while CANON section 6 says B in the air is the ground pound. The Stoop uses Down + Jump, so it works with either mapping, but the sim and canon should agree.
8. Not needed: no projectile, no new entity, no armor. Muzna is part of every sprite, not a sim object. The hurtbox stays 36 x 72 (target 64) like everyone else.
