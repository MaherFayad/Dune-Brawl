Dune Brawl: Full Development Plan

Oct 24, 2026 · @maher fayad

Goal and success criteria

Take Dune Brawl from today's prototype (live at dunebrawl.vercel.app,
rollback netcode working) to a shareable browser platform fighter with
Brawlhalla-level feel: real 2D characters made in Higgsfield, a full
combat system, painted stages, Arabic and English UI, and reliable
online play from a link. Art is proven first with 1 character and 4
animations before scaling.

In scope: game design, combat systems, art and animation pipeline,
renderer, stages, audio, UI and localization, netcode hardening,
hosting, QA, launch. Out of scope: Meshy and any 3D route, native mobile
apps, ranked ladders with accounts, monetization.

Game design

Dune Brawl is a 1v1 stock platform fighter: knock the rival off the
stage 3 times to win. Damage % raises knockback, so every hit builds
toward a kill. A match lasts 3 to 5 minutes and starts from a shared
link with no account.

Core loop: open link, pick fighter and palette, 3-2-1, fight, rematch in
one key press. Losing a match should take under 10 seconds to restart.

Match rules: 3 stocks, 8-minute timer (higher stocks then lower damage
wins), 3 jumps plus 1 recovery per airtime, blast zones fixed per stage,
items off. Both players can pick the same fighter; player 2 gets the
alternate palette automatically.

Data-driven moves: from phase 2, move data (frames, hitboxes, damage,
knockback, angles) moves out of code into one moves.json per fighter,
exported by the retiming editor. Both clients compare a hash of all move
data at handshake and refuse to start on mismatch, so a stale cache can
never cause a desync.

Readability rules: fighters face right in source art and are mirrored in
code; silhouettes must be recognizable as solid black shapes; no thin

game, bold outlines, cel shading). They never name Brawlhalla or its
characters, so the art is our own.

Retiming and hitbox editor

A small browser tool (/editor on the same site) turns each AutoSprite
sheet into game-ready animation and move data. It is the step that makes
AI art behave like fighting-game art.

1.  Import: drop in the sprite sheet PNG and its atlas JSON; frames load
    as a strip.

2.  Trim and pivot: auto-crop transparent edges; set the foot pivot once
    per animation (auto-detected from the lowest opaque row, then
    nudged).

3.  Retime: a 60 fps timeline shows startup, active and recovery bands
    from the move's frame data. Each game frame is assigned a source
    frame: hold, skip or repeat. Defaults spread frames evenly; the
    animator drags to hold the anticipation pose and put the smear frame
    on the first active frame.

4.  Hurtboxes: 1 to 3 rectangles per frame (head, body, legs); copy
    forward with one key.

5.  Hitboxes: drawn only on active frames; each carries damage, base
    knockback, growth, angle and an "away" flag.

6.  Preview: plays at true 60 fps beside a dummy, mirrored and
    unmirrored, with onion skin and a hitbox overlay toggle.

7.  Export: writes fighter.atlas.webp, fighter.anim.json (frame order
    per animation) and fighter.moves.json (frame data and boxes) plus a
    content hash.

Acceptance: retiming 1 attack takes under 5 minutes; the exported move
plays identically in the editor preview and in an online match.

Technical architecture

Each browser runs the complete game; only inputs and state hashes travel
between players. Servers only introduce players (signaling) and relay
traffic when a direct path fails (TURN).

Dune Brawl architecture · browser, services, tooling

The simulation is the single source of truth. Renderer, audio and
effects read its state every frame and never write to it, which is what
keeps rollback safe.

Codebase move (phase 1): from 4 loose files to Vite + TypeScript, with
the same no-framework approach. Layout:

/src/sim/ deterministic game rules (no floats, no clocks)
/src/net/ rollback session, peer connection, signaling client
/src/render/ PixiJS scenes, camera, effects
/src/audio/ sound triggers with rollback dedupe
/src/ui/ menus, HUD, i18n (en.json, ar.json)
/assets/fighters/ one folder per fighter: atlas, anim.json, moves.json
/assets/stages/ parallax layers + collision.json
/editor/ retiming and hitbox tool
/worker/ Cloudflare Worker: rooms + TURN credentials
/tests/ determinism, rollback soak, Playwright browser match

Rendering and engine integration

The Canvas 2D renderer is replaced by PixiJS (WebGL, version pinned at
build time). The simulation does not change: rendering only reads state,
never writes it.

Frame mapping rule: the sprite frame shown is a pure function of sim
state: frame = anim[state][t], where state and t come from the fighter
(for example ATK + move 1 + t 5 shows side light, game frame 5).
Rollback therefore re-renders the right pose for free, and no animation
clock ever runs on its own.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Item              Decision
  ----------------- -----------------------------------------------------
  Asset format      WebP atlas (2048 x 2048 max per page) + anim.json +
                    moves.json per fighter

  Sprite size       256 px source frames for gameplay; 512 px only for
                    menus and victory screens

  Palettes          Alternate palette as a second atlas generated in
                    Higgsfield, not a runtime shader, so colors stay
                    hand-picked

  Loading           Menu loads first (under 300 KB); fighters and stage
                    load while the lobby waits for the opponent

  Size budget       Under 3 MB per fighter, under 2 MB per stage, under 8
                    MB for a full match

  Effects           Procedural: hit bursts, rings, trails and dust drawn
                    with Pixi Graphics and particles

  Camera            Unchanged logic; Pixi container transform; 1 to 1
                    pixel snapping at rest to keep outlines crisp

  Fallback          If WebGL is missing, show a message rather than a
                    broken Canvas mode
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Determinism rules (unchanged and enforced in code review): no floats,
Math.random or clock reads in sim.js; move data only from hashed JSON;
renderer, audio and effects never write to sim state.

Audio (phase 4): hit sounds in 3 weights (light, heavy, kill), whoosh
per attack, jump, land, dodge, KO blast, menu sounds, one looping track
per stage. AutoSprite's with_sound option is tested on the first batch;
if quality falls short, use Higgsfield audio generation for effects and
music. Every sound is triggered from confirmed or predicted state with a
2-frame dedupe so rollback never plays a hit twice.

Effects (code, phase 1): comic hit bursts scaled by knockback, white
startup flash 2 frames before active, launch trail on tumbling fighters,
landing dust, dodge afterimage, KO blast toward stage center, damage %
shake on hit.

Launch

Launch is a link people send each other, so the sharing moment is the
funnel.

-   Address: maherfayad.com/brawl (ties to your portfolio and the 404
    game) or a short subdomain; the link preview image shows both
    fighters mid-clash with "I challenge you" in English and Arabic.

-   Share flow: the host screen offers Copy, WhatsApp and X share
    buttons; the guest lands straight in the lobby with no menu in
    between.

-   Analytics: cookieless, aggregate only: matches started, matches
    finished, rematch rate, connection failures by type, average ping.
    No user IDs, no fingerprinting.

-   PDPL (Saudi Personal Data Protection Law): the TURN relay and
    hosting process IP addresses, which can count as personal data.
    Publish a short bilingual privacy notice naming the processors, the
    purpose (connecting matches) and retention (relay logs only as long
    as the provider keeps them). Collect nothing else. Confirm the legal
    basis with counsel before any marketing push.

-   Soft launch: 20 friends and your network, 1 week, watch connection
    failures and the playtest survey; fix, then post publicly.

-   Showcase: a short case study on maherfayad.com covering the netcode,
    the AI art pipeline and the design decisions.
