# Dune Brawl: the world

The world bible. It expands `docs/design/CANON.md` and never contradicts it. On any single fighter, that fighter's bible in `docs/characters/` wins.

## 1. Logline

**EN:** Above the Empty Quarter the desert has broken into floating islands riding an endless storm, and once a year, when Suhail rises, champions fight in the storm's calm ring for the right to make one claim on it.

**AR:** فوق الربع الخالي تكسرت الصحراء إلى جزر طافية تحملها عاصفة لا تهدأ، وفي كل عام حين يطلع سهيل يتواجه الأبطال في حلقة السكون، ومن يصمد حتى النهاية ينال مطلبا واحدا من العاصفة.

## 2. The world

### The Empty Quarter

The Empty Quarter (الربع الخالي) was never empty to its people: hidden wells, a salt road from coast to mountains, date oases, falcon cliffs, pearl banks. Most of it is still on the ground. The parts that are not are the problem.

### The Drift

The Drift (المهب, from "في مهب الريح", at the mercy of the wind) is the floating islands and the storm that carries them: a dune, a caravan town, a night market, a stretch of cliff, all circling the Empty Quarter like a slow wheel, sand pouring from their roots in falls that never land.

One rule makes the Drift strange: **each island keeps the hour it was torn away.** Dune Island is forever at dusk, the Hejaz Ruins forever at noon, the Djinn Market forever at night, which the jinn consider a gift.

### The oath of the seven tribes

Long ago a storm marid came to the Empty Quarter as a guest and did not leave, and its summer would not end. The seven tribes could not drive it out, so they did the most desert thing possible: they hosted it. The marid ate their bread and salt, and nobody breaks faith with the people whose salt they have eaten. This is the **Oath of Bread and Salt** (عهد العيش والملح):

1. The marid sleeps beneath the sands, under a great tent held by **seven pegs** (الأوتاد السبعة), one driven by each tribe.
2. Every Night of Suhail, the hosts owe their guest a show: champions fighting in a ring of calm air.
3. The last one standing may ask the guest one thing. A guest who refuses a generous host is shamed, so the marid must answer.

The pegs are held by memory. When a tribe forgets its part, sends no champion or lets the feast go thin, its peg loosens. That is a crack, and every crack lifts more land.

### The storm marid: names

| Name | Arabic | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ajaj** | عجاج | The everyday Gulf word for a dust storm. Short, easy in English, easy to chant. Owned by no folk figure or scripture. | Plain; needs a title. |
| Haboob | هبوب | Known worldwide as the word for a desert dust wall. | Sounds like a weather report. |
| Abu Dawwama | أبو دوامة | "Father of the Whirl." Folkloric and warm. | Long, and reads as a nickname. |

**Recommendation: Ajaj (عجاج), "the Guest Beneath the Sand" (الضيف النائم تحت الرمل).** Gulf players already call a dusty sky "عجاج". Turning their weather word into a name they shout at a storm is the local wink the game wants, and the title carries the twist: Ajaj is not an evil to defeat but a guest who was asked to sleep and would rather not. Avoided on purpose: Simoom and Sarsar (scriptural echoes), Zawba'a (a jinn king of occult manuals).

### The Night of Suhail

In Gulf tradition Suhail (سهيل, Canopus) rising low in the south ends the summer heat: "إذا طلع سهيل برد الليل", when Suhail rises, the night cools. Dates finish ripening and the pearling fleet comes home.

The tribes chose that night because a summer marid is sleepiest when the heat breaks. On the night Suhail is due, the storm stills and opens a **calm ring** (حلقة السكون) at dusk. Champions fight through the dark, and in the last hour before dawn, when Suhail clears the horizon, the last one standing speaks the claim. Suhail is drawn alone, a gold point low in the south, never with a crescent.

## 3. Timeline

1. **The Summer Without End.** Ajaj arrives as a wind and stays as a guest. Wells drown in sand.
2. **The Oath of Bread and Salt.** About a thousand and one years ago the tribes host and peg the marid. The first Night of Suhail follows.
3. **The Long Calm.** For centuries the Night is the desert's best festival. Old claims are still told at fires: rain for a dry oasis, a lost caravan found.
4. **The Forgetting.** Two generations ago feasts thin and tribes stop sending champions. The first peg loosens and Dune Island rises. People start saying "the Drift".
5. **The Fall of the Forty.** A few years ago the Forty Thieves rob the oath feast's salt. A dancer of the caravan towns breaks the band. Their captain flees into the Drift and sells his name to the storm for passage.
6. **The Year of Lifting.** Last year the cracks come fast: the Hejaz Ruins, the falconers' cliffs, the Djinn Market. Ajaj starts drinking the sea into the sky.
7. **This year's Night of Suhail.** The ring opens wider than anyone has seen. A wider ring, the Star Guides say, means a stronger claim. Five champions answer.

## 4. The rules of the storm

| Game mechanic | In the world |
|---|---|
| Damage % | Every blow leaves storm sand on you. The number is how much you carry. |
| Knockback | Sand catches the wind like a sail: carry more, and the storm pulls harder when you are struck. |
| 3 stocks | The oath's courtesy: the storm carries a champion back three times. |
| Fourth fall | The storm keeps you. You wake in the dunes below with nothing, unhurt and embarrassed. |
| Respawn drop | The storm sets you down on a falling column of sand, a guest shown back to their seat. |
| Blast zones | The storm wall at the ring's edge. Each island tore loose differently, so each ring has its own size. |
| 8 minute timer | The calm lasts one breath of the marid. When Ajaj breathes out, the Star Guides judge: more returns left wins, then less sand. |
| Rematch | A host never refuses a guest who asks for more. Say "again" and the ring reopens. |
| Same fighter, two palettes | The storm will not hold two of one champion, so it paints the second in mirage colors: the Mirage Twin. Nobody can say which is real, and both are. |
| Practice vs bot | The Sand Double: packed storm sand in a champion's shape. Easy, normal and hard are how much wind the Star Guides pour in. |
| Training room | The Counting Tent, where the wind holds still. Pale dust shows where blows land (hitboxes), a drummer counts beats (frame data), sand shakes off at will (damage reset), and the Double stands as told (dummy settings). |
| Online from a link | A challenge flies by falcon. Whoever it finds may enter the ring. |
| Items off | The oath allows only what a champion carries in. |

## 5. Places

### Dune Island (جزيرة الكثيب)

- **Setting:** a wind-carved dune on red rock, locked at dusk. Broad main platform, two soft platforms of wind-held sandstone slabs.
- **History:** the first island to rise, once the Star Guides' camp. They still call the opening bouts here.
- **Painted layers:** purple to orange dusk with the south waiting for Suhail; the storm wall as a slow golden ring and islands trailing sandfalls; a neighbouring island of Star Guide tents and lantern-lit spectators; near rock spurs and one tent rope snapping in the wind, its peg pulled from nothing.
- **Mood:** warm sand underfoot, cold sky overhead, the world holding its breath.

### Hejaz Ruins (أطلال الحجاز)

- **Setting:** a red-gold sandstone caravan town, locked at noon. Main platform between two tall walls for clinging.
- **History:** the Salt Traders' market and caravan inn on the Salt Road, carved from Hejaz mountain stone. No tombs, no temples. It lifted mid-market, so salt sacks still sit on the stalls.
- **Painted layers:** white-hot noon and heat shimmer; a broken stretch of the Salt Road floating alone with a few loaded camels on it, unbothered; carved gateways and wooden lattice balconies over empty stalls; fallen arches and bleached awnings. No inscriptions on any stone.
- **Mood:** noon that never ends, hot stone, walls that remember caravans.

### Djinn Market (سوق الجن)

- **Setting:** a lantern-lit plaza, locked at night. One wide platform, three soft platforms of hovering awnings and a floating carpet.
- **History:** the jinn courts always traded with travellers on moonless nights. When the island lifted they called it a promotion. Now it opens nightly, prices are paid in things you will not miss (a yawn, a bad memory, your second-best shadow), and the crowd bets loudly.
- **Painted layers:** deep night with other islands hanging like lamps; the storm wall glowing faintly; jinn shoppers with glowing eyes and clear feet, stalls of bottled echoes and jarred moonlight; amber lantern strings, brass coffee pots, spice cones, a cat that is probably not a cat.
- **Mood:** everything is for sale, and the whole market is betting on you.

### Future stage ideas

- **The Hanging Sea (البحر المعلق):** a pearling dhow moored under a floating bubble of seawater. Deck as main platform, mast and yard as soft platforms, fish crossing the sky, divers diving up. The Sea Siren's home ground.
- **The Cave of the Forty (مغارة الأربعين):** the thieves' cave on its own island, its stone door opening and closing on the old words every 20 seconds and splitting the stage. Plain gold, no marked coins. The Nameless Captain's hideout.

## 6. Peoples and powers

### The seven tribes

All invented, none standing for a real tribe, family or country, each named for a craft rather than a lineage. They are told apart by tools and tent rope colors, never by emblems or writing.

| Tribe | Arabic | Who they are | Part in the oath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Star Guides | أدلاء النجوم | Night navigators and keepers of the calendar. | Chose the night. Now call Suhail, judge bouts, voice the announcer. |
| Well-Keepers | حفظة الآبار | Stern well-diggers who remember every debt. | Gave the water. |
| Salt Traders | أهل الملح | Caravan merchants of the Salt Road. | Gave the salt. |
| Palm Folk | أهل النخيل | Oasis growers. | Gave bread and dates; host the feast. |
| Sadu Weavers | أهل السدو | Weavers of tents and rope in bold geometric bands. | Wove the great tent and peg ropes. |
| Shell Coast Divers | أهل المحار | Pearl divers and their crews. | Brought pearls, the guest's gift. |
| Cliff Falconers | أهل الجروف | Falconers of the high cliffs. | Carried word of the oath by falcon. |

### The jinn courts (مجالس الجن)

Folk from the old tales: proud, funny, fond of bargains, devoted to fair play, neither good nor evil, never a matter of belief. They gather in courts as merchants gather in guilds: the **Court of Embers** (cooks and lamp-keepers), the **Court of Wells** (old, precise, keepers of water bargains), the **Court of the Night Wind** (messengers and gossips) and the **Court of Mirrors** (mirage-makers, who deny painting the Mirage Twin). The courts are split on the oath but never miss a bout. Which court, if any, the Djinn Warrior's djinn came from is that character bible's call.

### The Forty's remnant and the Nameless Captain

The Forty Thieves are not forty any more, and they are touchy about it. Their captain gave Ajaj his name in exchange for passage, and nobody can say it now, not even him. The **Nameless Captain** (القائد بلا اسم) sails the Drift on a ship with sails of woven sand, running the storm's errands: stealing salt, starting quarrels, loosening pegs. Free of the oath, Ajaj would owe its hosts nothing, and he means to be its only friend left. Only champions may enter the ring, so he buys claims instead. If `dagger-dancer.md` names or redraws him, that version wins.

### Pearl divers

The Shell Coast fleets dive all summer and come home in the **Qafal** (القفال), close to Suhail's rising. Each boat carries divers (Gulf **ghais**, غيص), pullers on the ropes, a captain and the **nahham** (النهام), who sets the work's rhythm with sea songs. This year half the pearl banks hang in the sky as hanging seas, and the bravest divers now dive upward.

### Falconers of the high cliffs

The Cliff Falconers raise great falcons said to descend from the Roc (الرخ) of Sindbad's voyages, and speak to them more gently than to their cousins. Last year their cliffs rose whole into the Drift. A cliff that drifts is not a cliff to a falcon, and the great birds have stopped nesting.

## 7. The five champions

**Djinn Warrior.** A desert swordsman who guards Salt Road caravans, bound to a djinn living in his sash and blade. It gives him speed and a sand-trailing scimitar, and never lets him forget who else is in the room. He wants the claim to end the bond; the djinn has its own plans for it. **Why this year:** the widest ring in memory means the strongest claim, strong enough, he hopes, to cut a bond that has outlasted every blade he tried on it.

**Ghoul of the Dunes.** For an age the deep desert's most patient liar: a voice behind the next dune, a campfire that was not there, a well that moved. It led caravans astray for the joy of it. Now the breaking oath is waking things older than the oath, and its desert keeps rising into the Drift. It wants the claim to keep its desert empty, quiet and its own. **Why this year:** this summer the sand under its favourite road rolled over like a sleeper, and for once the ghoul felt like the one being lured.

**Falcon Rider.** A falconer of the high cliffs, partnered with a great falcon of the Roc's line. The storm took the nesting cliffs, and the rider wants them brought down to earth. **Why this year:** the falcon will not nest on stone that moves, and a falcon that does not nest raises no young.

**Sea Siren of Sindbad.** A sea spirit from the waters Sindbad sailed, raised among the Shell Coast divers, with her songs from the nahham and her patience from the divers. The storm is drinking the sea into the sky; she wants it given back. **Why this year:** the fleet came home at the Qafal with empty baskets. She reached the Drift by swimming up through the hanging seas.

**Dagger Dancer.** A dancer and strategist of the caravan towns, inspired by the clever heroine of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves. When the thieves chalked the door they meant to rob, she chalked every door in the street. When they hid in oil jars, she sealed the lids and rolled the jars to the town watch. Their captain escaped to serve the storm, and she has come to finish it. **Why this year:** his ship has been seen at the storm wall, asking who might sell a claim, and she saw him talking with the Djinn Warrior's djinn.

## 9. The first season story: this Night of Suhail

**Act 1: The Rising (طلوع سهيل).** Dusk on Dune Island. The camps are the thinnest in memory; three tribes sent no one. The Star Guides call the Night and the ring opens so wide the crowd gasps. A ship with sails of sand circles just outside the storm wall.

**Act 2: The Offer (العرض).** Endless noon at the Hejaz Ruins. The Nameless Captain moves through the crowd making each champion's people one quiet offer: their claim, for what they want. The Dagger Dancer catches him beside the Djinn Warrior and strikes first. The Falcon Rider and the Sea Siren learn their claims cannot both come true.

**Act 3: The Pulled Peg (الوتد المنزوع).** Midnight at the Djinn Market. The island lurches: the captain's crew, lowered on ropes, has pulled the Salt Traders' peg far below, and a whole stall tumbles into the storm. Something under the Empty Quarter turns over in its sleep, and the Ghoul goes very still, because it knows the sound. The courts want to stop. The Star Guides refuse: leave the Night unfinished and every peg goes.

**Act 4: The Djinn's Voice (صوت الجني).** The last bouts before dawn. The djinn speaks to everyone for the first time: if its swordsman wins, it will speak the claim, and not for what he wants. It is not the captain's, the Dancer realises, but it is not the swordsman's either. Rivals stop trading insults and start trading warnings.

**Act 5: The Claim (المطلب).** The final, in the last dark. Suhail clears the horizon, gold and alone, and the storm goes silent to listen.

**Ending A: Bread and Salt Again (العيش والملح من جديد).** The Djinn Warrior stands last. The market is falling and the peg is gone, so instead of his freedom he claims the oath: hold yourself down. Charmed by a host this stubborn, Ajaj agrees to stand in for the missing peg for one year. The cracks stop; the bond stays; the djinn laughs for the first time, and nobody can tell if it is pleased. The captain sails off with the peg. Next Suhail, the storm's courtesy ends.

**Ending B: The Name (الاسم).** The Dagger Dancer stands last and claims the captain. The storm cannot give what is not its own, but it holds his name, so it gives her that. She speaks it and his ship drops out of the wind. He escapes on foot, but now she can find him anywhere. The peg is still out, more land rises, and the thing under the sand is awake.

**Maher picks.** A is the warmer close with a fixed deadline for season two. B is the sharper villain chase and a bigger world.

**Hook for the next fighters.** The Nameless Captain, hunted or holding a peg, steps into the ring himself with a sky ship and a crew of jars. The ghoul's elder kin, the Si'la (السعلاة), climbs out of the deep sand with a claim of its own. And under the Hanging Sea, divers report something big and grumpy: Bu Darya (بو دريا) of the Gulf's sea tales wants its water back too.

## 13. Voice and tone guide

**Do**
- Sound like a storyteller at a fire who loves the audience: theatrical, warm, quick to joke.
- Give everyone swagger, losers included. Boasts, not insults.
- Use concrete detail: salt, rope, lantern oil, hot stone, sand in your collar.
- Treat hospitality as the world's moral law: guests, hosts, bread and salt, debts.
- Use Gulf heritage words (Qafal, nahham, ghais, Sadu) with care, explained once.
- Twist cliches: not "an ancient evil awakens" but "an ancient guest wakes up and wants breakfast".

**Do not**
- Go grim. No horror, blood or death: people fall, get carried back, wake in the dunes.
- Use religious phrases in scripted lines, even casual ones. No scripture, prophets, Solomon, seals or stamped bottles.
- Pair Suhail with a crescent, or put writing or symbols on clothing, weapons or stone.
- Reach for Orientalist props: harems, slaves, "exotic" anything, genie jokes. Write "jinn".
- Villainize any real tribe, family or country, or name another game or its characters.
- Write English loanwords in Arabic letters where MSA has a good word.

## 14. Alternative premises

**A. The Thousand and Second Night.** A storyteller keeps a sleepless storm king entertained by telling him fights. Each match is a tale, the fighters are her characters, rematches are retellings and mirror matches are one hero told two ways. **For:** the tightest fit with One Thousand and One Nights, menus as chapters, any folk figure can join without plot debt. **Against:** stakes go soft because it is only a story, the Empty Quarter and Suhail lose weight, and a storyteller in a palace drifts toward Orientalist imagery. The canon gives players a place to stand; this gives them a voice to listen to.

**B. The Suhail Games.** Nothing is broken. The floating islands are a natural wonder, and when Suhail rises the tribes and jinn courts hold the games of summer's end, a heritage festival with a fighting ring at its heart. The prize is honor. **For:** bright and sporty, easy to frame seasons, ladders and new fighters, close to festivals GCC players know. **Against:** no villain, no arc, no reason this year matters, so the canon motives shrink into prizes. It sells the brawl and loses the myth.

## 15. Canon changes requested

**None required.** Three optional items for Maher:

1. **Hejaz Ruins name.** Hejaz is a real region holding the holiest cities, so "ruins" there may sit badly with some Saudi players. This file keeps the name and makes the place a caravan town with no tombs or worship sites. A fictional swap, "Salt Road Ruins" (أطلال طريق الملح), needs no other edits.
2. **Additions to adopt if accepted:** Ajaj; the Oath of Bread and Salt and its seven pegs; islands keeping the hour they were torn away; Star Guides as judges and announcer; the claim spoken when Suhail clears the horizon; المهب for the Drift.
3. **Plan note (not canon):** the development plan's Arabic UI table calls for Saudi dialect and uses loanwords (أونلاين، البوت، كيبورد), against the canon MSA rule. Possible fixes: "إنشاء مباراة عبر الإنترنت", "تدريب ضد شبيه الرمل", "لاعبان على لوحة مفاتيح واحدة".
