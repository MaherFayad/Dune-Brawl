# Dune Brawl: the world

This is the world bible. It agrees with `docs/design/CANON.md` and only expands it. The five character bibles in `docs/characters/` own each fighter's details; where this file touches a fighter, it stays inside the canon seeds, and the character bible wins if the two ever differ on that fighter.

## 1. Logline

**EN:** Above the Empty Quarter the desert has broken into floating islands riding an endless storm, and once a year, when Suhail rises, champions fight in the storm's calm ring for the right to make one claim on it.

**AR:** فوق الربع الخالي تكسرت الصحراء إلى جزر طافية تحملها عاصفة لا تهدأ، وفي كل عام حين يطلع سهيل يتواجه الأبطال في حلقة السكون، ومن يصمد حتى النهاية ينال مطلبا واحدا من العاصفة.

## 2. The world

### The Empty Quarter

The Empty Quarter (الربع الخالي) was never empty to the people who lived in it. It had wells only the Well-Keepers could find, a salt road that ran from the coast to the mountains, oases heavy with dates, cliffs full of falcons, and a coast where divers went down for pearls every summer. It is still there, far below. Most of it is still on the ground. The parts that are not are the problem.

### The Drift

The Drift (المهب, from the phrase "في مهب الريح", at the mercy of the wind) is the name for the floating islands and the storm that carries them, taken together. The islands are torn pieces of the old desert: a dune, a caravan town, a night market, a stretch of cliff. They hang in a slow, wide storm that circles the Empty Quarter like a wheel and never stops turning. Sand pours off their roots in long falls that never reach the ground.

One rule makes the Drift strange: **each island keeps the hour it was torn away.** Dune Island lifted at dusk and has been at dusk ever since. The Hejaz Ruins lifted at noon and still bake under a noon sun. The Djinn Market lifted at night, and the jinn were delighted. Travellers set their day by which island they stand on.

### The oath of the seven tribes

Long ago a storm marid came to the Empty Quarter as a guest and did not leave. Its summer would not end. Wells filled with sand and caravans walked in circles. The seven tribes could not drive it out, so they did the most desert thing possible: they hosted it.

They spread a feast, and the marid ate their bread and salt. That made the pact binding, because in the desert nobody breaks faith with the people whose salt they have eaten. This is the **Oath of Bread and Salt** (عهد العيش والملح). Its terms were simple:

1. The marid sleeps beneath the sands, under a great tent woven by the Sadu Weavers and held by **seven pegs** (الأوتاد السبعة), one driven by each tribe.
2. Every year, on the Night of Suhail, the tribes owe their guest a show: champions who fight for its entertainment in a ring of calm air.
3. The last champion standing may ask the guest one thing in return. A guest who refuses a generous host is shamed, so the marid must answer.

The pegs are held by memory. When a tribe forgets its part, sends no champion, or lets the feast go thin, its peg loosens. That is a crack. Each crack lifts more land into the sky.

### The storm marid: names

| Name | Arabic | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ajaj** | عجاج | An everyday Gulf word for a dust storm, so GCC players get it at once. Two short syllables, easy in English, easy to chant. Belongs to no folklore figure and no scripture. | Plain on paper, so it needs a title to sound grand. |
| Haboob | هبوب | Known worldwide as the meteorology word for a desert dust wall. | Sounds like a weather report. Generic. |
| Abu Dawwama | أبو دوامة | "Father of the Whirl." The kunya form feels folkloric, warm and a little funny. | Long. Reads as a nickname, so it softens the menace too much. |

**Recommendation: Ajaj (عجاج), full title "Ajaj, the Guest Beneath the Sand" (عجاج، الضيف النائم تحت الرمل).** The word is already in the player's mouth. In Kuwait, eastern Saudi Arabia and across the Gulf, people say the sky is "عجاج" on a dusty day. Turning a word they use about the weather into a name they shout at a boss is exactly the kind of local wink the game wants. The title carries the twist of the setting: Ajaj is not an evil to be defeated. It is a guest who was asked to sleep and would rather not.

Avoided on purpose: Simoom and Sarsar, which carry scriptural echoes, and Zawba'a, which belongs to the old jinn king lists of occult manuals.

### The Night of Suhail

In Gulf tradition the rising of Suhail (سهيل, Canopus) low in the southern sky marks the end of the worst summer heat. The old saying goes "إذا طلع سهيل برد الليل": when Suhail rises, the night cools. Dates finish ripening, the pearling fleet comes home, and people breathe again.

The tribes chose that night for the oath because a summer marid is at its sleepiest when the heat breaks. So every year, on the night Suhail is due, the storm stills. At dusk it opens a **calm ring** (حلقة السكون) around the islands, and the champions fight through the dark. In the last hour before dawn, when Suhail itself clears the southern horizon, the last one standing speaks the claim. The star is always drawn alone: one bright, slightly golden point low in the south, never paired with a crescent.

## 3. Timeline

1. **The Summer Without End.** Ajaj arrives as a wind and stays as a guest. One summer lasts years. Wells drown in sand.
2. **The Oath of Bread and Salt.** About a thousand and one years ago, the seven tribes host the marid, share bread and salt with it, and peg it beneath the sands. The first Night of Suhail is fought that same year.
3. **The Long Calm.** For centuries the Night is the best festival in the desert. Famous claims are still told around fires: rain for a dry oasis, a lost caravan found, a well that never sinks.
4. **The Forgetting.** Two generations ago, feasts thin and some tribes stop sending champions. The first peg loosens. Dune Island tears free at dusk and floats up. People start saying "the Drift".
5. **The Fall of the Forty.** A few years ago the Forty Thieves rob the salt stores meant for the oath feast. A dancer of the caravan towns breaks the band with a trick. Their captain escapes into the Drift and sells his name to the storm for passage.
6. **The Year of Lifting.** Last year the cracks come fast: the Salt Traders' caravan town (the Hejaz Ruins) at noon, the falconers' high cliffs whole, the Djinn Market by night. Ajaj begins drinking the sea into the sky.
7. **This year's Night of Suhail.** The calm ring opens wider than anyone has ever seen. The Star Guides say a wider ring means a stronger claim. Five champions answer.

## 4. The rules of the storm

| Game mechanic | In the world |
|---|---|
| Damage % | Every blow leaves storm sand on you. The number is how much sand you carry; the Star Guides read it from the glow. |
| Knockback | Sand catches the wind like a sail. The more you carry, the harder the storm pulls when you are struck. |
| 3 stocks | The oath's courtesy: the storm carries a champion back to the ring three times. |
| Fourth fall | The storm keeps you. You wake in the dunes below with nothing, unhurt and embarrassed, and walk home. Nobody dies. |
| Respawn drop | The storm sets you down from above on a falling column of sand, like a guest shown back to their seat. |
| Blast zones | The storm wall at the edge of the calm ring. Touch it and you are taken. Each island tore loose differently, so each ring has its own size. |
| 8 minute timer | The calm lasts one breath of the marid: eight minutes. When Ajaj must breathe out, the Star Guides judge. Whoever the storm returned fewer times wins; if equal, whoever carries less sand. |
| Rematch | A host never refuses a guest who asks for more. Say "again" and the ring reopens before the dust settles. |
| Same fighter, two palettes | The storm will not hold two of one champion, so it paints the second in mirage colors: the Mirage Twin (توأم السراب). Nobody can say which is the real one, and both are. |
| Practice vs bot | The Sand Double (شبيه الرمل): storm sand packed into a champion's shape by the Star Guides. Easy, normal and hard are how much wind they pour into it. |
| Training room | The Counting Tent (خيمة العد): a tent where the wind is held still. Pale dust shows where every blow can land (hitbox view), a drummer counts each beat (frame data), you shake the sand off at will (damage reset), and you tell the Double how to stand (dummy settings). |
| Online from a link | A challenge flies by falcon. Whoever the falcon finds may step into the ring. |
| Local versus | Two champions from the same tent, sharing one fire. |
| Items off | The oath allows only what a champion carries in. |

## 5. Places

### Dune Island (جزيرة الكثيب)

**Setting.** A wind-carved dune on a raft of red rock, locked at dusk. One broad main platform and two soft platforms of wind-held sandstone slabs.

**History.** The first island to rise, torn loose by the first crack two generations ago. It was the Star Guides' camping ground, and they still call the opening bouts here.

**Painted layers.**
- Sky: dusk from deep purple to orange, the first stars, the southern horizon empty and waiting for Suhail.
- Far: the storm wall as a slow golden ring, other islands drifting with sandfalls pouring from their roots.
- Middle: a neighbouring island with the Star Guides' tents, lanterns and a crowd of spectators.
- Near: wind-polished rock spurs and one tent rope snapping in the wind, its peg pulled from nothing.

**Mood.** Warm sand underfoot, cold sky overhead, and the whole world holding its breath.

### Hejaz Ruins (أطلال الحجاز)

**Setting.** A caravan town of red-gold sandstone, stuck at noon. A main platform between two tall walls built for clinging.

**History.** The Salt Traders' caravan town on the old Salt Road, carved from Hejaz mountain stone. A market and a caravan inn with a courtyard. No tombs, no temples. It lifted during last year's cracks while the market was open, so salt sacks still sit on the stalls.

**Painted layers.**
- Sky: white-hot noon, almost no shadows, heat shimmer on the horizon.
- Far: a broken stretch of the Salt Road floating alone, with a few loaded camels standing on it, unbothered.
- Middle: carved gateways, balconies with wooden lattice screens, empty stalls with salt sacks and rope.
- Near: fallen arches and sun-bleached awnings. No inscriptions on any stone.

**Mood.** Noon that never ends: short shadows, hot stone, and walls that remember caravans.

### Djinn Market (سوق الجن)

**Setting.** A lantern-lit night market on a wide stone plaza, locked at night. One wide platform and three soft platforms made of hovering stall awnings and a floating carpet.

**History.** The jinn courts have traded with travellers on moonless nights for as long as anyone can remember. When the market island lifted, the courts took it as a promotion. Now it is open every night, prices are paid in things you will not miss (a yawn, a bad memory, your second-best shadow), and the crowd bets loudly on the champions.

**Painted layers.**
- Sky: deep night, with other lit islands hanging in the dark like lamps.
- Far: the storm wall glowing faintly where lantern light hits the sand.
- Middle: a crowd of jinn shoppers with glowing eyes and clear feet, stalls selling bottled echoes and jars of moonlight, a carpet seller whose stock hovers an inch off the ground.
- Near: strings of amber lanterns, brass coffee pots, spice cones, a cat that is probably not a cat.

**Mood.** Everything is for sale, the lanterns never run out of oil, and the whole market is betting on you.

### Future stage ideas

- **The Hanging Sea (البحر المعلق).** A pearling dhow moored beneath a floating bubble of seawater the storm drank from the coast. The deck is the main platform, the mast and yard are soft platforms, and fish swim across the sky behind. Divers in the background dive up, not down. Home ground for the Sea Siren and the pearl divers.
- **The Cave of the Forty (مغارة الأربعين).** The thieves' cave on an island of its own, its great stone door opening and closing on the old words every 20 seconds, splitting the stage. Piles of plain gold and glass, no coins with marks. The Nameless Captain's hideout, and the natural stage for him if he becomes a fighter.

## 6. Peoples and powers

### The seven tribes

All seven are invented. None stands for a real tribe, family or country, and each is named for its craft, not a lineage.

| Tribe | Arabic | Who they are | Part in the oath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Star Guides | أدلاء النجوم | Night navigators who read the sky for direction and season. Keep the calendar. | Chose the night. Today they call Suhail, judge the bouts and are the voice of the announcer. |
| Well-Keepers | حفظة الآبار | Stern diggers of the deep wells. Remember every debt. | Gave the water of the feast. |
| Salt Traders | أهل الملح | Caravan merchants of the Salt Road. Their town is now the Hejaz Ruins. | Gave the salt. Their stores were what the Forty robbed. |
| Palm Folk | أهل النخيل | Oasis growers. Their dates ripen as Suhail rises. | Gave the bread and dates. Host the feast. |
| Sadu Weavers | أهل السدو | Weavers of tents and rope in bold geometric bands. | Wove the great tent over the sleeping marid and the ropes of the seven pegs. |
| Shell Coast Divers | أهل المحار | Pearl divers and their crews. | Brought pearls, the guest's gift. |
| Cliff Falconers | أهل الجروف | Falconers of the high cliffs. | Carried word of the oath to every camp by falcon. |

The tribes are told apart by their tools and by the color of their tent ropes, never by emblems or writing on clothing.

### The jinn courts (مجالس الجن)

The jinn of the Drift are folk from the old tales: proud, funny, fond of bargains and devoted to fair play. They are not good or evil, and nothing about them is a matter of belief. They gather in courts, the way merchants gather in guilds.

- **The Court of Embers:** cooks, smiths and lamp-keepers. They run the market's food stalls and keep the lanterns lit.
- **The Court of Wells:** quiet, old and precise. They live at the bottoms of deep wells and keep water bargains to the drop.
- **The Court of the Night Wind:** messengers and gossips. If something was said in the Drift, they have heard it twice.
- **The Court of Mirrors:** mirage-makers. They paint the Mirage Twin, though they deny it.

The courts are split on the oath. Some like the Drift, with its endless night market. Some fear what a free Ajaj would do. All of them love the Night of Suhail and never miss a bout. Which court, if any, the djinn in the Djinn Warrior's sash came from is the character bible's call.

### The Forty's remnant and the Nameless Captain

The Forty Thieves are not forty any more, and they are touchy about it. The remnant still answers to their captain, who fled into the Drift after the dancer broke the band. To get the storm to carry him, he gave Ajaj the only thing it wanted: his name. Nobody can say it now, not even him.

Now he is the **Nameless Captain** (القائد بلا اسم). He sails the Drift on a sky ship with sails of woven sand and runs errands for the storm. His plan is to help the tribes forget: steal their salt, set them quarrelling, loosen their pegs. Free of the oath, Ajaj would owe its hosts nothing, and he means to be the only friend it has left. He cannot fight in the ring, because the oath admits only champions. So he buys claims instead. If `dagger-dancer.md` gives the captain a name or a different look, that version wins.

### Pearl divers

The Shell Coast fleets go out for the summer and come home in the **Qafal** (القفال), the return of the fleet near summer's end, close to Suhail's rising. Each boat has divers (the Gulf word is **ghais**, غيص), pullers who haul them up by rope, a captain, and a singer, the **nahham** (النهام), who keeps the rhythm of the work with sea songs. This year the storm has drunk half the pearl banks into the sky as hanging seas. The bravest divers now dive upward.

### Falconers of the high cliffs

The Cliff Falconers raise great falcons said to come from the line of the Roc (الرخ), the giant bird of Sindbad's voyages. They train with a feathered lure and speak to their birds more gently than to their cousins. Last year their cliffs rose into the Drift whole. A cliff that drifts is not a cliff to a falcon, and the great birds have stopped nesting.

## 7. The five champions

**Djinn Warrior.** A desert swordsman who guards caravans on the Salt Road, bound to a djinn that lives in his sash and blade. The bond gives him speed and a scimitar that trails sand, and it never lets him forget who else is in the room. He wants to win the claim and end the bond. The djinn agreed to come along far too quickly, and it has its own plans for the claim. **Why this year:** the ring is the widest anyone remembers, and a wider ring means a stronger claim. Strong enough, he hopes, to cut a bond that has outlasted every blade he has tried on it.

**Ghoul of the Dunes.** For a long age the ghoul was the most patient liar in the deep desert: a voice behind the next dune, a campfire that was not there, a well that moved when you reached it. It led caravans astray for the joy of it. Now the breaking of the oath is waking things older than the oath under the deep sand, and pieces of its desert keep rising into the Drift. It wants the claim to keep its desert: empty, quiet and its own. **Why this year:** this summer the sand under its favourite road rolled over like a sleeper, and for the first time the ghoul felt like the one being lured.

**Falcon Rider.** A falconer of the high cliffs, partnered with a great falcon of the Roc's line. Last year's crack lifted the nesting cliffs into the storm. The rider wants them brought down to earth again. **Why this year:** the great falcon will not nest on stone that moves. A falcon that does not nest raises no young, and the line of the Roc does not get a second chance.

**Sea Siren of Sindbad.** A sea spirit from the waters Sindbad sailed, raised among the Shell Coast divers. She learned her songs from the nahham and her patience from the divers. The storm is drinking the sea into the sky, and she wants it given back. **Why this year:** the fleet came home at the Qafal with empty baskets. She reached the Drift by swimming up through the hanging seas.

**Dagger Dancer.** A dancer and strategist of the caravan towns, inspired by the clever heroine of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves. When the thieves chalked a mark on the door they meant to rob, she chalked every door in the street the same. When they hid in oil jars, she sealed the lids and rolled the jars to the town watch. Their captain escaped and now serves the storm. She has come to finish it. **Why this year:** the captain's sand-sailed ship has been seen at the storm wall, and he has been asking which champions might sell a claim. She saw him talking with the djinn in the Djinn Warrior's sash.

## 9. The first season story: this Night of Suhail

### Act 1: The Rising (طلوع سهيل)
Dusk on Dune Island. The camps are thinner than any year in memory; three tribes sent no one at all. The Star Guides call the Night, and the calm ring opens so wide the crowd gasps. A ship with sails of sand circles just outside the storm wall. The first bouts begin, and the five champions size each other up.

### Act 2: The Offer (العرض)
Endless noon at the Hejaz Ruins. The Nameless Captain walks among the spectators, making the same quiet offer to each champion's people: your claim for what you want, delivered by the storm. The Dagger Dancer catches him beside the Djinn Warrior and attacks first. The Falcon Rider and the Sea Siren learn that their claims cannot both come true.

### Act 3: The Pulled Peg (الوتد المنزوع)
Midnight at the Djinn Market. The island lurches. The captain's crew, lowered on ropes from the ship, has pulled the Salt Traders' peg from the sand far below. The market drops a whole stall into the storm. Something under the Empty Quarter turns over in its sleep, and the Ghoul goes very still, because it recognises the sound. The jinn courts want to stop the Night. The Star Guides refuse: if the Night is not finished, every peg goes. So the champions keep fighting.

### Act 4: The Djinn's Voice (صوت الجني)
The last bouts before dawn. For the first time, the djinn speaks aloud to everyone: if its swordsman wins, it will speak the claim, and it will not ask for what he wants. The Dagger Dancer realises the djinn is not the captain's. It is not the swordsman's either. Rivals stop trading insults and start trading warnings.

### Act 5: The Claim (المطلب)
The final, in the last dark before dawn. Suhail clears the southern horizon, gold and alone, and the storm goes silent to listen.

### Ending A: Bread and Salt Again (العيش والملح من جديد)
The Djinn Warrior stands last. He came to end his bond, but the market is falling and the pulled peg is gone. He claims the oath instead: hold yourself down. Ajaj, delighted by a host this stubborn, agrees to stand in for the missing peg itself for one year. The cracks stop. The Drift stays aloft. The bond stays too, and the djinn laughs for the first time; nobody can tell if it is pleased. The captain sails off with the peg. Next Suhail, the storm's courtesy ends.

### Ending B: The Name (الاسم)
The Dagger Dancer stands last. She claims the captain. The storm cannot give what is not its own, but it holds one thing of his, so it gives her that: his name. She speaks it aloud, and his ship falls out of the wind. He escapes on foot into the Drift, but now she can find him anywhere. The peg is still out. The cracks go on, more land rises, and the thing under the sand is awake.

**Maher picks one.** A gives a warmer close and a fixed deadline for season two. B gives a sharper villain chase and a bigger world.

### Hook for the next fighters
The Nameless Captain, now hunted or now holding a peg, is ready to step into the ring as a champion with a sky ship and a crew of jars. Below, the ghoul's older kin, the Si'la (السعلاة), climbs out of the deep sand with a claim of its own. And on the Hanging Sea the divers report something big and grumpy under the dhow: Bu Darya (بو دريا), the old sea creature of Gulf tales, wants its water back too.

## 13. Voice and tone guide

**Do**
- Write like a storyteller at a fire who loves the audience: theatrical, warm, quick to joke.
- Give swagger to everyone, even the losers. Boasts, not insults.
- Use concrete desert detail: salt, rope, lantern oil, hot stone, sand in your collar.
- Lean on hospitality as the moral law of the world: guests, hosts, bread and salt, debts.
- Use Gulf heritage words (Qafal, nahham, ghais, Sadu) with care and explain them once.
- Twist the cliche instead of using it: not "an ancient evil awakens" but "an ancient guest wakes up and would like breakfast".

**Do not**
- Go grim. No horror, no blood, no death. In lore, people fall, are carried back, are taken by the storm, wake in the dunes.
- Use religious phrases, even casual ones, in scripted lines. No scripture, prophets, Solomon or seals, no bottles stamped with marks.
- Pair Suhail with a crescent, or put writing or symbols on clothing, weapons or stone.
- Reach for old Orientalist props: harems, sultans with slaves, "exotic" anything, genie jokes. Write "jinn", not "genie".
- Make any real tribe, family or country the bad guy, or name another game or its characters.
- Put English loanwords in Arabic letters when MSA has a good word.

## 14. Alternative premises

**A. The Thousand and Second Night.** A storyteller keeps a sleepless storm king entertained by telling him fights. Every match is a tale told aloud, the fighters are characters from her stories, and the claim is the ending she lets the winner choose. Rematches are retellings and mirror matches are the same hero told two ways. **For:** the tightest possible fit with One Thousand and One Nights, a natural home for menus as chapters, and room to add any folk figure without plot debt. **Against:** stakes go soft because it is only a story, the Empty Quarter and Suhail lose their weight unless grafted on, and a storyteller in a palace drifts toward the Orientalist palace imagery the guide warns against. The canon premise gives players a place to stand; this one gives them a voice to listen to.

**B. The Suhail Games.** Nothing is broken. The floating islands are a natural wonder, and every year when Suhail rises, the tribes and the jinn courts hold the great games of summer's end, like a heritage festival with a fighting ring at its heart. The prize is honor and a trophy, not a claim on a storm. **For:** bright and sporty, very easy to frame seasons, ladders and new fighters, and it mirrors the festival culture GCC players know. **Against:** no villain, no arc and no reason why this year matters, so the canon motives (ending a bond, saving a sea, keeping a desert) shrink into prizes. It sells the brawl and loses the myth.
