# Dune Brawl: the world

The world bible. It agrees with `docs/design/CANON.md` and only expands it. The character bibles in `docs/characters/` own each fighter; where this file touches a fighter it stays inside the canon seeds, and the character bible wins on that fighter.

## 1. Logline

**EN:** Above the Empty Quarter the desert has broken into floating islands riding an endless storm, and once a year, when Suhail rises, champions fight in the storm's calm ring for the right to make one claim on it.

**AR:** فوق الربع الخالي تكسرت الصحراء إلى جزر طافية تحملها عاصفة لا تهدأ، وفي كل عام حين يطلع سهيل يتواجه الأبطال في حلقة السكون، ومن يصمد حتى النهاية ينال مطلبا واحدا من العاصفة.

## 2. The world

### The Empty Quarter

The Empty Quarter (الربع الخالي) was never empty to the people who lived in it: wells only the Well-Keepers could find, a salt road from the coast to the mountains, oases heavy with dates, cliffs full of falcons, a coast where divers went down for pearls every summer. Most of it is still on the ground. The parts that are not are the problem.

### The Drift

The Drift (المهب, from "في مهب الريح", at the mercy of the wind) is the floating islands and the storm that carries them. The islands are torn pieces of the old desert: a dune, a caravan town, a night market, a stretch of cliff. The storm circles the Empty Quarter like a slow wheel, and sand pours off the islands' roots in falls that never reach the ground.

One rule makes the Drift strange: **each island keeps the hour it was torn away.** Dune Island lifted at dusk and is still at dusk. The Hejaz Ruins still bake at noon. The Djinn Market lifted at night, and the jinn were delighted.

### The oath of the seven tribes

Long ago a storm marid came to the Empty Quarter as a guest and did not leave. Its summer would not end. The seven tribes could not drive it out, so they did the most desert thing possible: they hosted it. The marid ate their bread and salt, and in the desert nobody breaks faith with the people whose salt they have eaten. This is the **Oath of Bread and Salt** (عهد العيش والملح):

1. The marid sleeps beneath the sands, under a great tent woven by the Sadu Weavers and held by **seven pegs** (الأوتاد السبعة), one driven by each tribe.
2. Every year, on the Night of Suhail, the hosts owe their guest a show: champions fighting for its entertainment in a ring of calm air.
3. The last champion standing may ask the guest one thing. A guest who refuses a generous host is shamed, so the marid must answer.

The pegs are held by memory. When a tribe forgets its part, sends no champion or lets the feast go thin, its peg loosens. That is a crack, and each crack lifts more land into the sky.

### The storm marid: names

| Name | Arabic | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ajaj** | عجاج | The everyday Gulf word for a dust storm. Short, easy in English, easy to chant. Owned by no folklore figure or scripture. | Plain on paper; needs a title to sound grand. |
| Haboob | هبوب | Known worldwide as the word for a desert dust wall. | Sounds like a weather report. |
| Abu Dawwama | أبو دوامة | "Father of the Whirl." Folkloric, warm, a little funny. | Long, and reads as a nickname, so it softens the menace. |

**Recommendation: Ajaj (عجاج), titled "the Guest Beneath the Sand" (الضيف النائم تحت الرمل).** Across the Gulf people already say the sky is "عجاج" on a dusty day. Turning a word they use about the weather into a name they shout at a storm is the local wink the game wants, and the title carries the setting's twist: Ajaj is not an evil to defeat. It is a guest who was asked to sleep and would rather not. Avoided on purpose: Simoom and Sarsar (scriptural echoes) and Zawba'a (a jinn king of occult manuals).

### The Night of Suhail

In Gulf tradition the rising of Suhail (سهيل, Canopus) low in the southern sky marks the end of the summer heat: "إذا طلع سهيل برد الليل", when Suhail rises, the night cools. Dates finish ripening and the pearling fleet comes home.

The tribes chose that night because a summer marid is sleepiest when the heat breaks. So every year, on the night Suhail is due, the storm stills. At dusk it opens a **calm ring** (حلقة السكون) around the islands and champions fight through the dark. In the last hour before dawn, when Suhail clears the horizon, the last one standing speaks the claim. Suhail is always drawn alone, one gold point low in the south, never with a crescent.

## 3. Timeline

1. **The Summer Without End.** Ajaj arrives as a wind and stays as a guest. One summer lasts years and wells drown in sand.
2. **The Oath of Bread and Salt.** About a thousand and one years ago, the seven tribes host the marid and peg it beneath the sands. The first Night of Suhail is fought that year.
3. **The Long Calm.** For centuries the Night is the desert's best festival. Old claims are still told at fires: rain for a dry oasis, a lost caravan found.
4. **The Forgetting.** Two generations ago feasts thin and tribes stop sending champions. The first peg loosens, Dune Island rises at dusk, and people start saying "the Drift".
5. **The Fall of the Forty.** A few years ago the Forty Thieves rob the salt meant for the oath feast. A dancer of the caravan towns breaks the band with a trick. Their captain flees into the Drift and sells his name to the storm for passage.
6. **The Year of Lifting.** Last year the cracks come fast: the Salt Traders' town (the Hejaz Ruins), the falconers' cliffs, the Djinn Market. Ajaj begins drinking the sea into the sky.
7. **This year's Night of Suhail.** The calm ring opens wider than anyone has seen. A wider ring, the Star Guides say, means a stronger claim. Five champions answer.

## 4. The rules of the storm

| Game mechanic | In the world |
|---|---|
| Damage % | Every blow leaves storm sand on you. The number is how much you carry; the Star Guides read it from its glow. |
| Knockback | Sand catches the wind like a sail. The more you carry, the harder the storm pulls when you are struck. |
| 3 stocks | The oath's courtesy: the storm carries a champion back to the ring three times. |
| Fourth fall | The storm keeps you. You wake in the dunes below with nothing, unhurt and embarrassed, and walk home. |
| Respawn drop | The storm sets you down from above on a falling column of sand, a guest shown back to their seat. |
| Blast zones | The storm wall at the ring's edge. Touch it and you are taken. Each island tore loose differently, so each ring has its own size. |
| 8 minute timer | The calm lasts one breath of the marid. When Ajaj breathes out, the Star Guides judge: fewer returns used wins, then less sand. |
| Rematch | A host never refuses a guest who asks for more. Say "again" and the ring reopens before the dust settles. |
| Same fighter, two palettes | The storm will not hold two of one champion, so it paints the second in mirage colors: the Mirage Twin. Nobody can say which is real, and both are. |
| Practice vs bot | The Sand Double: storm sand packed into a champion's shape. Easy, normal and hard are how much wind the Star Guides pour into it. |
| Training room | The Counting Tent, where the wind is held still. Pale dust shows where blows land (hitbox view), a drummer counts each beat (frame data), you shake off sand at will (damage reset) and tell the Double how to stand (dummy settings). |
| Online from a link | A challenge flies by falcon. Whoever it finds may step into the ring. |
| Items off | The oath allows only what a champion carries in. |

## 5. Places

### Dune Island (جزيرة الكثيب)

**Setting.** A wind-carved dune on a raft of red rock, locked at dusk. A broad main platform and two soft platforms of wind-held sandstone slabs.
**History.** The first island to rise. It was the Star Guides' camping ground, and they still call the opening bouts here.
**Painted layers.** Sky: purple to orange dusk, first stars, the south waiting for Suhail. Far: the storm wall as a slow golden ring, islands trailing sandfalls. Middle: a neighbouring island of Star Guide tents, lanterns and spectators. Near: polished rock spurs and one tent rope snapping in the wind, its peg pulled from nothing.
**Mood.** Warm sand underfoot, cold sky overhead, the world holding its breath.

### Hejaz Ruins (أطلال الحجاز)

**Setting.** A caravan town of red-gold sandstone, locked at noon. A main platform between two tall walls built for clinging.
**History.** The Salt Traders' town on the Salt Road, carved from Hejaz mountain stone: a market and a caravan inn around a courtyard. No tombs, no temples. It lifted while the market was open, so salt sacks still sit on the stalls.
**Painted layers.** Sky: white-hot noon, heat shimmer. Far: a broken stretch of the Salt Road floating alone, a few loaded camels standing on it, unbothered. Middle: carved gateways, balconies with wooden lattice screens, empty stalls. Near: fallen arches and bleached awnings. No inscriptions on any stone.
**Mood.** Noon that never ends: short shadows, hot stone, walls that remember caravans.

### Djinn Market (سوق الجن)

**Setting.** A lantern-lit market on a wide stone plaza, locked at night. One wide platform and three soft platforms of hovering stall awnings and a floating carpet.
**History.** The jinn courts traded with travellers on moonless nights for as long as anyone remembers. When the island lifted, the courts called it a promotion. Now it opens every night, prices are paid in things you will not miss (a yawn, a bad memory, your second-best shadow), and the crowd bets loudly on champions.
**Painted layers.** Sky: deep night, other lit islands hanging like lamps. Far: the storm wall glowing where lantern light hits it. Middle: jinn shoppers with glowing eyes and clear feet, stalls of bottled echoes and jarred moonlight, a carpet seller whose stock hovers an inch up. Near: amber lantern strings, brass coffee pots, spice cones, a cat that is probably not a cat.
**Mood.** Everything is for sale, the lanterns never run dry, and the whole market is betting on you.

### Future stage ideas

- **The Hanging Sea (البحر المعلق).** A pearling dhow moored under a floating bubble of seawater the storm drank from the coast. Deck as main platform, mast and yard as soft platforms, fish swimming across the sky, divers diving up. Home ground for the Sea Siren.
- **The Cave of the Forty (مغارة الأربعين).** The thieves' cave on its own island. Its stone door opens and closes on the old words every 20 seconds, splitting the stage. Plain gold and glass, no marked coins. The Nameless Captain's hideout.

## 6. Peoples and powers

### The seven tribes

All invented, none standing for a real tribe, family or country, each named for a craft rather than a lineage. They are told apart by tools and tent rope colors, never by emblems or writing on clothing.

| Tribe | Arabic | Who they are | Part in the oath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Star Guides | أدلاء النجوم | Night navigators who read the sky for direction and season. | Chose the night. Now call Suhail, judge bouts and voice the announcer. |
| Well-Keepers | حفظة الآبار | Stern diggers of deep wells who remember every debt. | Gave the water. |
| Salt Traders | أهل الملح | Caravan merchants of the Salt Road. | Gave the salt the Forty later robbed. |
| Palm Folk | أهل النخيل | Oasis growers whose dates ripen as Suhail rises. | Gave the bread and host the feast. |
| Sadu Weavers | أهل السدو | Weavers of tents and rope in bold geometric bands. | Wove the great tent and the peg ropes. |
| Shell Coast Divers | أهل المحار | Pearl divers and their crews. | Brought pearls, the guest's gift. |
| Cliff Falconers | أهل الجروف | Falconers of the high cliffs. | Carried word of the oath by falcon. |

### The jinn courts (مجالس الجن)

The jinn of the Drift are folk from the old tales: proud, funny, fond of bargains, devoted to fair play. Not good or evil, and never a matter of belief. They gather in courts the way merchants gather in guilds:

- **Court of Embers:** cooks and lamp-keepers who run the market's food stalls.
- **Court of Wells:** old and precise, living at the bottoms of wells, keeping water bargains to the drop.
- **Court of the Night Wind:** messengers and gossips. Anything said in the Drift, they heard twice.
- **Court of Mirrors:** mirage-makers who paint the Mirage Twin, though they deny it.

The courts are split on the oath. Some love an endless night market, some fear a free Ajaj, and all of them never miss a bout. Which court the djinn in the Djinn Warrior's sash came from, if any, is the character bible's call.

### The Forty's remnant and the Nameless Captain

The Forty Thieves are not forty any more, and they are touchy about it. The remnant still answers to their captain. To get the storm to carry him, he gave Ajaj the one thing it asked: his name. Nobody can say it now, not even him.

The **Nameless Captain** (القائد بلا اسم) sails the Drift on a ship with sails of woven sand and runs errands for the storm. His plan is to help the tribes forget: steal their salt, set them quarrelling, loosen their pegs. Free of the oath, Ajaj would owe its hosts nothing, and he means to be its only friend left. The oath admits only champions to the ring, so he buys claims instead. If `dagger-dancer.md` names or redraws him, that version wins.

### Pearl divers

The Shell Coast fleets go out all summer and come home in the **Qafal** (القفال), close to Suhail's rising. Each boat carries divers (the Gulf word is **ghais**, غيص), pullers who haul them up by rope, a captain, and the **nahham** (النهام), a singer who keeps the work's rhythm with sea songs. This year the storm drank half the pearl banks into the sky as hanging seas, and the bravest divers now dive upward.

### Falconers of the high cliffs

The Cliff Falconers raise great falcons said to descend from the Roc (الرخ), the giant bird of Sindbad's voyages. They train with a feathered lure and speak to their birds more gently than to their cousins. Last year their cliffs rose whole into the Drift, and a cliff that drifts is not a cliff to a falcon. The great birds have stopped nesting.

## 7. The five champions

**Djinn Warrior.** A desert swordsman who guards Salt Road caravans, bound to a djinn that lives in his sash and blade. The bond gives him speed and a scimitar that trails sand, and never lets him forget who else is in the room. He wants the claim to end the bond. The djinn agreed to come along far too quickly, and it has its own plans for the claim. **Why this year:** the widest ring in memory means the strongest claim, strong enough, he hopes, to cut a bond that has outlasted every blade he tried on it.

**Ghoul of the Dunes.** For an age the ghoul was the most patient liar in the deep desert: a voice behind the next dune, a campfire that was not there, a well that moved when you reached it. It led caravans astray for the joy of it. Now the breaking oath is waking things older than the oath, and pieces of its desert keep rising into the Drift. It wants the claim to keep its desert empty, quiet and its own. **Why this year:** this summer the sand under its favourite road rolled over like a sleeper, and for the first time the ghoul felt like the one being lured.

**Falcon Rider.** A falconer of the high cliffs, partnered with a great falcon of the Roc's line. Last year's crack lifted the nesting cliffs into the storm, and the rider wants them brought down to earth again. **Why this year:** the falcon will not nest on stone that moves. A falcon that does not nest raises no young, and the Roc's line does not get a second chance.

**Sea Siren of Sindbad.** A sea spirit from the waters Sindbad sailed, raised among the Shell Coast divers. She learned her songs from the nahham and her patience from the divers. The storm is drinking the sea into the sky, and she wants it given back. **Why this year:** the fleet came home at the Qafal with empty baskets. She reached the Drift by swimming up through the hanging seas.

**Dagger Dancer.** A dancer and strategist of the caravan towns, inspired by the clever heroine of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves. When the thieves chalked the door they meant to rob, she chalked every door in the street the same. When they hid in oil jars, she sealed the lids and rolled the jars to the town watch. Their captain escaped and now serves the storm, and she has come to finish it. **Why this year:** his sand-sailed ship has been seen at the storm wall, asking which champions might sell a claim, and she saw him talking with the djinn in the Djinn Warrior's sash.

## 9. The first season story: this Night of Suhail

**Act 1: The Rising (طلوع سهيل).** Dusk on Dune Island. The camps are the thinnest in memory; three tribes sent no one. The Star Guides call the Night and the ring opens so wide the crowd gasps. A ship with sails of sand circles just outside the storm wall. The five champions size each other up.

**Act 2: The Offer (العرض).** Endless noon at the Hejaz Ruins. The Nameless Captain walks among the spectators making each champion's people the same quiet offer: your claim, for what you want, delivered by the storm. The Dagger Dancer catches him beside the Djinn Warrior and strikes first. The Falcon Rider and the Sea Siren learn their claims cannot both come true.

**Act 3: The Pulled Peg (الوتد المنزوع).** Midnight at the Djinn Market. The island lurches: the captain's crew, lowered on ropes, has pulled the Salt Traders' peg from the sand far below. A whole stall tumbles into the storm. Something under the Empty Quarter turns over in its sleep, and the Ghoul goes very still, because it knows the sound. The courts want to stop the Night. The Star Guides refuse: leave the Night unfinished and every peg goes.

**Act 4: The Djinn's Voice (صوت الجني).** The last bouts before dawn. For the first time the djinn speaks to everyone: if its swordsman wins, it will speak the claim, and it will not ask for what he wants. The Dancer sees the djinn is not the captain's. It is not the swordsman's either. Rivals stop trading insults and start trading warnings.

**Act 5: The Claim (المطلب).** The final, in the last dark. Suhail clears the southern horizon, gold and alone, and the storm goes silent to listen.

**Ending A: Bread and Salt Again (العيش والملح من جديد).** The Djinn Warrior stands last. He came to end his bond, but the market is falling and the peg is gone, so he claims the oath instead: hold yourself down. Ajaj, charmed by a host this stubborn, agrees to stand in for the missing peg itself for one year. The cracks stop and the Drift stays aloft. So does the bond, and the djinn laughs for the first time; nobody can tell if it is pleased. The captain sails off with the peg, and next Suhail the storm's courtesy ends.

**Ending B: The Name (الاسم).** The Dagger Dancer stands last and claims the captain. The storm cannot give what is not its own, but it holds one thing of his, so it gives her that: his name. She speaks it aloud and his ship falls out of the wind. He escapes on foot, but now she can find him anywhere. The peg is still out, the cracks go on, and the thing under the sand is awake.

**Maher picks.** A is the warmer close with a fixed deadline for season two. B is the sharper villain chase and a bigger world.

**Hook for the next fighters.** The Nameless Captain, hunted or holding a peg, is ready to enter the ring himself with a sky ship and a crew of jars. Below, the ghoul's elder kin, the Si'la (السعلاة), climbs out of the deep sand with a claim of its own. And under the Hanging Sea, divers report something big and grumpy: Bu Darya (بو دريا), the old sea creature of Gulf tales, wants its water back too.

## 13. Voice and tone guide

**Do**
- Write like a storyteller at a fire who loves the audience: theatrical, warm, quick to joke.
- Give everyone swagger, losers included. Boasts, not insults.
- Use concrete desert detail: salt, rope, lantern oil, hot stone, sand in your collar.
- Treat hospitality as the world's moral law: guests, hosts, bread and salt, debts.
- Use Gulf heritage words (Qafal, nahham, ghais, Sadu) with care, explained once.
- Twist cliches: not "an ancient evil awakens" but "an ancient guest wakes up and would like breakfast".

**Do not**
- Go grim. No horror, blood or death. People fall, get carried back, are taken by the storm, wake in the dunes.
- Use religious phrases in scripted lines, even casual ones. No scripture, prophets, Solomon, seals or stamped bottles.
- Pair Suhail with a crescent, or put writing or symbols on clothing, weapons or stone.
- Reach for Orientalist props: harems, slaves, "exotic" anything, genie jokes. Write "jinn".
- Make any real tribe, family or country the villain, or name another game or its characters.
- Write English loanwords in Arabic letters where MSA has a good word.

## 14. Alternative premises

**A. The Thousand and Second Night.** A storyteller keeps a sleepless storm king entertained by telling him fights. Every match is a tale, the fighters are her characters, and the claim is the ending she lets the winner choose; rematches are retellings and mirror matches are one hero told two ways. **For:** the tightest fit with One Thousand and One Nights, menus as chapters, and any folk figure can join without plot debt. **Against:** stakes go soft because it is only a story, the Empty Quarter and Suhail lose their weight, and a storyteller in a palace drifts toward the Orientalist imagery this guide warns against. The canon gives players a place to stand; this gives them a voice to listen to.

**B. The Suhail Games.** Nothing is broken. The floating islands are a natural wonder, and when Suhail rises the tribes and jinn courts hold the great games of summer's end, a heritage festival with a fighting ring at its heart. The prize is honor, not a claim on a storm. **For:** bright and sporty, easy to frame seasons, ladders and new fighters, and close to the festival culture GCC players know. **Against:** no villain, no arc and no reason this year matters, so the canon motives (ending a bond, saving a sea, keeping a desert) shrink into prizes. It sells the brawl and loses the myth.

## 15. Canon changes requested

**None required.** Three optional items for Maher:

1. **Hejaz Ruins name.** Hejaz is a real region, home to the holiest cities, so "ruins" there could sit badly with some Saudi players. This file keeps the name and makes the place a caravan town with no tombs or worship sites. If Maher wants a fictional name, "Salt Road Ruins" (أطلال طريق الملح) works with no other edits.
2. **Additions to fold into canon if accepted:** the name Ajaj; the Oath of Bread and Salt and its seven pegs; islands keeping the hour they were torn away; Star Guides as judges and announcer; the claim spoken when Suhail clears the horizon before dawn; المهب as the Arabic name of the Drift.
3. **Development plan note (not canon):** its Arabic UI table calls for Saudi dialect and uses loanwords (أونلاين، البوت، كيبورد), against the canon MSA rule. Possible fixes: "إنشاء مباراة عبر الإنترنت", "تدريب ضد شبيه الرمل", "لاعبان على لوحة مفاتيح واحدة".
